# Build your own trans-Tasman receipt corpus

There is no public, labelled receipt dataset from New Zealand or Australia, so you will have to build the test set yourself. The well-known receipt benchmarks come from elsewhere: SROIE is Asian with 6% GST lines, CORD is Indonesian and ships no merchant or date labels, and ExpressExpense is US restaurant receipts. None of them exercises NZD/AUD, 15%/10% GST, GST numbers or ABNs, or day-first dates. The only documented Australian collection is a 118-receipt research set from Melbourne, and nothing says it was ever released. The approach that works for this library has three layers. First, a seeded TypeScript generator that writes NZ/AU receipt *text* together with its expected fields. Second, about 100–200 real receipts per country, redacted, run through ML Kit and Apple Vision on a device, with the OCR JSON committed instead of the photos. Third, free vendor OCR tiers used only to pre-label the real receipts before a person checks them. The NZ-only assumptions matter more than they look. On an Australian supermarket docket, GST is often far below a fixed fraction of the total because basic food is GST-free. Cash rounds to 5c in Australia but 10c in New Zealand. Both countries' rules make the GST number, the "Tax Invoice" heading and the GST line optional on small receipts. The test set has to hit each of these on purpose, along with the traps that put several total-like figures near the word "total".

## Every public receipt benchmark misses NZ and AU

The public datasets are useful for testing the general OCR-to-fields pipeline, but none of them tests anything specific to NZ or AU. **SROIE (ICDAR 2019)** has about 1,000 scanned receipts labelled with company, address, date and total, from establishments "primarily located in Asia" dated around 2015–2018 ([Voxel51/scanned_receipts](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Voxel51/scanned_receipts)). Sample annotations show "GST@6%" lines, which fits Malaysia's 2015–2018 GST, though no primary source I found says "Malaysia" outright ([Kaggle derivative](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/elfuegodev/receiptdatasetssd300v2-csv)). **CORD** has 1,000 Indonesian receipts under CC BY 4.0, but `store_info` and `payment_info` were stripped "due to Indonesian legal issues". That leaves **no merchant or date labels**, only menu items, subtotal/tax and total ([clovaai/cord](https://github.com/clovaai/cord)). **WildReceipt** has 1,740 English receipts with 25 key-value categories, and I found no licence or country of origin ([arXiv 2103.14470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.14470)). **ExpressExpense SRD** is 200 unlabelled restaurant receipts under MIT from a Californian company ([ExpressExpense](https://expressexpense.com/blog/free-receipt-images-ocr-machine-learning-dataset/)). The TrainingDataPro/UniqueData grocery set is a paid sample under **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0**, which rules it out for a commercial test suite, and the no-derivatives term even forbids your own annotations ([HF TrainingDataPro](https://huggingface.co/datasets/TrainingDataPro/ocr-receipts-text-detection)).

Searches turned up nothing specific to the region. No Kaggle, Hugging Face or Roboflow set of Woolworths/Countdown, New World, PAK'nSAVE, Bunnings or Coles receipts surfaced. The closest find is the **118 Australian receipts** behind a 2025 Textract evaluation: 77 phone photos and 41 e-receipt screenshots, from 59 vendors led by Woolworths (19), Woolworths Metro (10) and Coles (7). There are no formal labels, and the paper gives no link or licence ([arXiv 2512.19958](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19958v1)). Its capture breakdown is still a useful template for your own set: 62 clear and 15 blurred, 35 folded, 24 crumpled, 52 skewed. `wooliesR` scrapes your *own* Woolworths AU e-receipts into JSON but ships no samples ([ekutilov/wooliesR](https://github.com/ekutilov/wooliesR)). OCR vendors don't fill the gap either. Azure's receipt model lists an **en-AU locale but no en-NZ**, and its only sample is a generic Contoso receipt ([Azure language support](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/document-intelligence/language-support/prebuilt)). Taggun documents ABN extraction with checksum validation, and NZD/GST for New Zealand, without publishing sample images ([Taggun Australia](https://developers.taggun.io/docs/australia); [Taggun NZ](https://developers.taggun.io/docs/new-zealand)). This search was not exhaustive: Zenodo, data.govt.nz, data.gov.au and Roboflow were not searched directly.

If you want a non-local regression set, CORD (CC BY 4.0) and ExpressExpense (MIT) are the only clearly redistributable options. Commit a few attributed samples, or only their derived OCR text, and fetch the rest at test time. Don't vendor SROIE or WildReceipt. The Voxel51 mirror labels SROIE CC BY 4.0, and a third-party table says MIT, but the original competition terms couldn't be checked because the official site failed TLS ([Voxel51](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Voxel51/scanned_receipts); [Bee corpus paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.13795)). Be careful using these sets as NZ/AU proxies. Their date orders and tax labels ("GST 6%", "Sales Tax") would teach your tests the wrong assumptions.

## Ten receipt features decide whether the library works in both countries

The tax rules set the baseline. In **New Zealand**, since 1 April 2023 the law asks for "taxable supply information" rather than a formal tax invoice, but retailers may keep printing "Tax Invoice" ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/taxable-supply-information-for-gst)). Requirements depend on the amount. At **$200 or less**, a receipt needs only the seller's name, the date, a description and the amount, with **no GST number**. Above $200, the GST number becomes mandatory, plus either the excl/GST/incl amounts or the inclusive total with a statement that GST is included, the latter only when every item is standard-rated. Above $1,000 for a registered buyer, the buyer's name and an identifier are also required ([IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/gst/tax-invoices-for-gst/how-tax-invoices-for-gst-work)). One research note summarised the middle band as always needing the exclusive and GST amounts, but the IRD page explicitly allows the "GST included" alternative, so the generator should produce both. In **Australia**, a tax invoice is legally required only for sales of **$82.50 or more**. Below $1,000 it shows "Tax Invoice", the seller's name and ABN, the date, item descriptions, quantity and price, and either the GST or "total price includes GST". At $1,000 and above it also identifies the buyer ([Xero AU](https://www.xero.com/au/guides/tax-invoice-requirements/); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/australian-tax-invoice-requirements)). The ATO site returned 403 to automated fetches, so these rules come from paraphrases that agree with each other rather than from verbatim ATO text.

The table below lists what the test data must cover. The library's `gstFromInclusive` in `/Users/nathandalpatan/Documents/tech/remi/src/money.ts` already takes a rate in basis points (1500 gives 3/23, 1000 gives 1/11). The bigger risk is the logic built on top of it, which assumes GST is a fixed fraction of the total.

| Feature | New Zealand | Australia | Why it breaks an extractor |
|---|---|---|---|
| GST rate | 15%, 3/23 of inclusive total | 10%, 1/11 of inclusive total | Hard-coded NZ rate. Use worked cents cases: NZ 4567→596, AU 5000→455 (454.5 rounds up) |
| Mixed supply | Rare in retail | **Routine at supermarkets**: GST-free basic food | A Woolworths docket of tea, coffee and milk totalling $33.00 carried **$2.65 GST, not $3.00** ([Dealer Solutions](https://help.dealersolutions.com.au/Accounting/add_taxable__non_taxable_compo.htm)). All-GST-free dockets print $0.00 or no GST line ([OzBargain](https://ozbargain.com.au/node/230016)) |
| Tax ID | IRD number, 8–9 digits, mod-11, max raised to 200,000,000 in 2026 ([python-stdnum](https://arthurdejong.org/python-stdnum/doc/stdnum.nz.ird); [IRD](https://www.ird.govt.nz/updates/news-folder/2026/increase-to-ird-number-validation-upper-limit)) | ABN, 11 digits in 2-3-3-3 groups, mod-89 ([ABR](https://abr.business.gov.au/Help/AbnFormat)) | Digit runs mistaken for amounts. Optional on small receipts |
| Cash rounding | Nearest **10c**. 5c endings at retailer discretion, usually down ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_rounding)) | Nearest **5c** (1–2c down, 3–4c up) ([WA Consumer Protection](https://www.consumerprotection.wa.gov.au/consumer-protection/rounding)) | Rounded vs unrounded total. One note wrongly said "10c in both countries" |
| Card surcharges | Still legal; the ban bill had stalled as of 2026 ([Eftpos NZ](https://blog.eftpos.co.nz/blog/2026-surcharge-ban-guide-for-nz-businesses)) | Removed on eftpos/Visa/Mastercard from **1 Oct 2026**; Amex was left for review ([RBA](https://rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-10.html)) | The EFTPOS total exceeds the till total |
| Dates | Day-first | Day-first; Tyro prints "17 Feb 2024 at 06:40 PM" ([Tyro](https://docs.integrated-eftpos.tyro.com/integrated-eftpos/iclient/implementation-guide/integrated-receipts.md)) | 03/04 must parse as 3 April |
| Loyalty and savings | Everyday Rewards (since Feb 2024); **Club+** replaced Clubcard on 15 Jun 2026 ([Inside Retail](https://insideretail.co.nz/2024/02/01/woolworths-launches-everyday-rewards-loyalty-program-into-nz/); [Foodstuffs](https://www.foodstuffs.co.nz/news-room/2026/new-world-pak-nsave-four-square-launch-club---a-smarter-way-to-save-on-groceries)) | Everyday Rewards lines sit under "Total Savings" ([Finder](https://www.finder.com.au/woolworths-rewards-faq)); Flybuys | Savings and balances read as totals |
| Merchant naming | Countdown became **Woolworths** (completed Dec 2025) ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolworths_New_Zealand)); macronised place names | Franchise and operator names | Old vs new brand. Māori macrons dropped or mangled by OCR |
| Tips and weekend surcharges | Less common | Must be separate lines ([Tyro](https://docs.integrated-eftpos.tyro.com/integrated-eftpos/iclient/implementation-guide/integrated-receipts.md)); 10–15% weekend/holiday surcharges are lawful ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.com.au/articles/public-holiday-surcharges-in-australian-cafes-essential-compliance-guide/)) | The till total differs from the amount charged |
| Small-receipt omissions | No GST number or "Tax Invoice" at or under $200 | No tax invoice needed under $82.50 | Missing fields must give null, not a guess |

Some current-events items need re-checking before you rely on them. Nobody confirmed that card networks actually enforced the AU no-surcharge rules on 1 October 2026. NAB guidance reportedly says Amex and UnionPay are covered too, while the RBA text names only eftpos, Mastercard and Visa ([RBA](https://rba.gov.au/media-releases/2026/mr-26-10.html)). Whether NZ's surcharge ban passed later in 2026 is also unknown. Since users scan old receipts, the fixtures should include surcharges on pre-October-2026 AU receipts either way.

## Most extraction failures come from figures that sit next to "total"

The sourced rules prove these features exist. How a given retailer prints them is unverified: no real Woolworths, Coles, PAK'nSAVE, Bunnings or cafe receipt images or transcriptions were found in any source. The trap cases below are therefore design inferences, and that is the strongest argument for collecting real receipts.

Cash receipts are the most common trap. An NZ cash docket reading `TOTAL 23.47 / Rounding +0.03 / CASH 50.00 / CHANGE 26.50` and an AU one reading `SUBTOTAL 23.47 / ROUNDING -0.02 / TOTAL 23.45 / CASH 50.00 / CHANGE 26.55` show the same purchase rounding in opposite directions. Neither the tendered nor the change amount is the total. Card payments cause the same problem in a different way: the till prints one figure, then the EFTPOS slip adds `SURCHARGE 0.45` or `TIP 5.00` and prints a larger `TOTAL`. The library needs a written policy on which figure it stores, the till total or the amount charged, and fixtures for both. GST adds its own traps: `GST 15%`, where 15 is a rate and not an amount; a GST-exclusive subtotal on the same line as the word GST; and GST numbers like `123-456-789`. Two edge cases matter most for GST. The first is a fixture where the printed GST is **1c off** 3/23 or 1/11, because the POS summed GST per line; the extractor must prefer the printed figure. The second is the AU mixed docket where printed GST is $1.82 on $64.30 (1/11 would be $5.85); there, computing GST from the total is simply wrong. Smaller traps round out the set. Savings and loyalty lines (`TOTAL SAVINGS $12.40`, `Club+ Dollars balance $15.00`) sit near the total. A customer copy and a merchant copy in one photo duplicate the totals. Refunds print negatives as `-29.98`, `29.98-` or `(29.98)`. Fuel dockets carry `189.9c/L` and litre figures. Receipts over $1,000 carry a buyer block, and in AU that means a second ABN that must not be taken for the merchant's.

The current extractor in `/Users/nathandalpatan/Documents/tech/remi/src/extract.ts` will be tested on a few of these points right away. GST is taken as the smallest amount on a GST-mentioning line that is below the total, which handles exclusive-subtotal distractors. The textual date pattern needs whitespace and a four-digit year, so EFTPOS-style `02NOV26` and `02-Nov-2026` forms are worth including. I have not run those inputs through it.

## Build the corpus in three layers, with photos kept out of git

**Layer one is a seeded text generator inside Vitest.** The library takes OCR text, so a generator that renders receipt *text* gives the most coverage per hour. Something like `generateReceipt({country, seed})` would return `{ocrText, expected}`, with the expected values in integer cents. Faker (MIT) has `en_AU` and `en_NZ` locales for names and addresses. The generator varies the dimensions in the table above: GST wording, tax-ID format and checksum validity, presence or absence of the "Tax Invoice" heading, threshold boundaries ($82.50, $200, $1,000), date formats, rounding, surcharges, tips and loyalty lines. Tax IDs should be random but checksum-valid, never real businesses'. Image tools are optional: NAVER's SynthDoG (MIT) and SynthTIGER are Python image generators, worth adding only if the OCR or PDF stages need image-level tests ([clovaai/donut](https://github.com/clovaai/donut); [PyPI synthtiger](https://pypi.org/project/synthtiger/1.1.0/)). One design rule is essential. The generator must not compute AU GST as total/11 on mixed dockets, because that would reproduce exactly the bug the tests exist to catch. Assign a tax status to each item and sum per line.

**Layer two is real receipts, captured once on a device as OCR JSON.** Collect your own receipts plus those of consenting family and friends. Aim for **100–200 per country**: at 90% accuracy that gives a 95% confidence interval of roughly ±6 points at n=100 and ±4 points at n=200 (standard binomial arithmetic, not a sourced recommendation). Cover supermarkets, fuel, hospitality, pharmacy, hardware, discount department stores and e-receipts in both countries. Redact masked card numbers, auth codes, loyalty numbers, customer names, phone numbers and medical items. Business names, addresses and tax IDs are public and can stay. Keep the photos in private storage. ML Kit v2 returns blocks, lines, elements and symbols with boxes and confidence ([ML Kit](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/text-recognition/v2)), and Apple's new `RecognizeDocumentsRequest` returns hierarchical document structure ([WWDC25 session 272](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/272/)). Store each engine's full output with its geometry as `fixtures/{country}/{id}/ocr.mlkit.json` and `ocr.vision.json`, next to a hand-checked `expected.json`, and record the engine and OS versions. Capturing with both engines matters because they group lines and confuse characters (O/0, S/5, $/S) differently. For image-quality variants, run Augraphy (MIT) offline for folds, shadows, glare and photocopy degradation ([sparkfish/augraphy](https://github.com/sparkfish/augraphy)), then OCR the outputs and commit only the JSON. Avoid AlbumentationsX, which is AGPL or commercial; the original MIT Albumentations was archived in July 2025 ([albumentations](https://github.com/albumentations-team/albumentations)). Augraphy has no thermal-fade effect, and long receipts can't be simulated convincingly, so both need real samples. Long receipts also exercise the PDF packing of multi-photo captures.

**Layer three is vendor pre-labelling, verified by hand.** The free tiers together cover a few hundred receipts a month: Azure Document Intelligence F0 at 500 pages ([Azure pricing](https://azure.microsoft.com/ja-jp/pricing/details/ai-document-intelligence/)), Mindee at about 250 ([Mindee](https://mindee.com/product/receipt-ocr-api)), Textract AnalyzeExpense at 100 for three months for new accounts ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/pricing/)), and Veryfi at about 100 ([Veryfi](https://veryfi.com:443/pricing)). Those figures change often, and Mindee's own docs contradict each other. Accept a field automatically when two vendors agree and the GST arithmetic checks out, and review everything else by hand. Vendor accuracy also gives you a cloud baseline to compare the on-device library against. One caution: sending other people's receipts to US-hosted services counts as disclosure overseas under NZ IPP 12 and AU APP 8, so the consent note must say so ([OPC NZ](https://www.privacy.org.nz/privacy-principles/); [OAIC](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles/australian-privacy-principles-quick-reference)). Vendor data-retention and model-training terms were not checked.

Score the results per field, per country and per capture condition. Match amounts in exact cents, with no ±1c tolerance, because a tolerance hides exactly the rounding bugs this corpus is meant to catch. Match dates as exact ISO strings and flag DD/MM swaps separately. Report abstentions (a null field) separately from confident wrong answers, because a wrong total does more harm than a missing one. Keep a held-out set of real receipts that is never looked at while writing heuristics.

## Conclusion

The missing dataset turns out to be a manageable problem, and in one way a useful one. Building the set yourself means designing it around the cases that would actually break this library, and generic benchmarks would never surface the biggest one: on Australian supermarket dockets, GST is often not a fixed fraction of the total. Supporting both countries is therefore more than swapping 15% for 10%. Printed GST must be treated as authoritative, and the rate formula should serve only as an upper-bound sanity check, a change that also helps the occasional mixed NZ receipt.

The other implication concerns time. Retail details in both countries changed during 2023–2026: the Countdown rebrand, Club+, the AU surcharge removal, the NZ TSI regime and the IRD number range. A test set frozen at one date will slowly drift away from what users scan. Date-stamp every fixture, keep pre-change variants alongside current ones, and treat the real-receipt layer as something you top up regularly rather than a one-time collection.
